Privacy Policy

Effective date: 23 November 2026 · Contact: privacy@hopnetapp.com

HopNet is an end-to-end encrypted messenger with no accounts, no phone number and no email sign-up. You add contacts yourself, in person by QR code or with an invite link you send. Your messages, photos, videos, voice notes, files, calls and group conversations are encrypted on your phone, and only the people you are talking to hold the keys. We cannot read your messages or see your contacts, and we keep no record of who you talk to.

Who we are

HopNet is published by Anton Shifman, the seller shown on the App Store. Questions about this policy or your data go to privacy@hopnetapp.com.

What we collect

We do not collect personal information. HopNet has no accounts and no user database, and contains no analytics, no advertising and no trackers. We cannot look anyone up, because there is nothing to look up. The few things that do leave your phone are described below: encrypted data passing through a "blind mailbox", the technical data any internet connection exposes, and what you choose to send us.

What stays on your phone

Your identity keys are kept in the iPhone's secure storage (the Keychain). Your contacts, your message history and a local diagnostics log are stored in HopNet's private storage on your phone, protected by your iPhone's own device encryption and passcode. You can add a Face ID lock to the app (App Lock), and you can erase everything at any time with Settings → Clear All Data.

How messages travel

Everything you send is end-to-end encrypted (X3DH key agreement and the Double Ratchet, standard published algorithms) before it leaves your phone. It then travels one of two ways:

  • Directly between phones, when both phones are online and the network allows a direct connection. To find each other, phones use a distributed hash table (DHT), and on the same Wi-Fi they use the local network (this is why the app asks for Local Network access). A direct connection means your IP address is visible to the contact's phone you connect to and to the public DHT nodes that help phones find each other. If you need to hide your IP address, use a VPN. With a VPN, phones often cannot connect directly, so messages and media go through the blind mailbox and can be slower.
  • Through the blind mailbox, when there is no direct connection, for example because your contact's app is closed or the network blocks direct links.

The blind mailbox

The blind mailbox is a small relay service hosted for us by Cloudflare, acting as our processor. It carries encrypted messages, encrypted photos and other media, delivery and read receipts, and the signals that set up calls. It is "blind" because:

  • It stores only data that is already end-to-end encrypted. The keys exist only on your phone and your contacts' phones; the mailbox, Cloudflare and HopNet cannot read the contents.
  • Items are filed under box identifiers that look like random numbers. Your phone and your contact's phone compute them from a secret only the two of you share; they contain no name, and neither the mailbox nor we can work back from them to your keys or your contacts. The same identifier is used again for the same conversation, so the mailbox can see that a box is in use, not whose it is. The mailbox holds no names, numbers or accounts and keeps no record of who is messaging whom. Like any internet service, it does see the IP address of each request (see below).
  • Messages, receipts and call signals are padded to a few fixed sizes, so their length reveals little. Photos, videos and files are not padded, so the mailbox can see their approximate size.
  • Items are deleted once they are delivered; anything left (for example media in a group, which every member has to fetch) is deleted automatically after 7 days at most.

What the mailbox and Cloudflare can technically observe is limited to encrypted data and its size, timestamps, the box identifiers, the HopNet app version making the request, and the IP address of the request. Cloudflare, as our hosting provider, processes IP addresses in order to serve each request; our own code does not record IP addresses or content. To control costs and stop abuse, the service keeps usage counters: for each request, the first characters of the box identifier, the type of operation and the app build number. Nothing else.

Notifications

To tell you about a new message or call while the app is closed, HopNet uses Apple's Push Notification service. If you allow notifications, your phone registers an Apple device token with the blind mailbox, stored next to your box identifiers (only requests that come from the HopNet app are accepted) and refreshed by the app; tokens that are not refreshed are deleted after 30 days. Every notification says only "HopNet — New message", also for calls, and carries the box identifier the app should check: no content and no sender. Apple delivers it, and so sees the device token and the timing, as for any app. You can turn notifications off in iOS Settings; messages then arrive when you open the app.

Voice and video calls

Calls are end-to-end encrypted (WebRTC with DTLS-SRTP). When the two phones cannot connect directly, the encrypted audio and video pass through a relay (TURN) server run by Cloudflare. The relay forwards encrypted media it cannot decode; like any internet service it sees the IP addresses, timing and data volume of the call, never the content. Your phone gets relay credentials valid for one hour from the blind mailbox, and only requests that come from the HopNet app are accepted.

Groups

Group conversations are end-to-end encrypted in the same way: each message travels as a separate encrypted envelope to each member. Group membership is kept on the members' phones; no group exists on any server.

Adding contacts and invite links

The most private way to add a contact is to scan their QR code in person; that exchange never touches any server. You can also share an invite link. An invite link carries only your public contact information, your display name and public keys (the same data the QR code shows). It never contains your private keys or any message.

By default the invite link is a short web link: your public contact information is stored by the blind mailbox under a random identifier and deleted automatically within 30 days. For these links the provider can observe that an invite was created and opened, the IP address of the request, and your public contact information. If you prefer that invites never touch our servers, turn on Settings → Private invite links: the link then carries its data only in the part of the address that browsers never send to a server, and the recipient pastes it instead of tapping it.

Treat an invite link like a key to your front door: anyone who obtains it can add you and see your public profile until it expires. Share it only with people you want to hear from.

iCloud backup

You can back up HopNet to your own iCloud account. Your identity keys are kept in your iCloud Keychain. Your chat history can be backed up to your iCloud Drive as an encrypted archive (AES-256-GCM); the backup key is created on your phone and kept in your iCloud Keychain. Apple stores only the encrypted archive, and HopNet has no copy of the key or the archive. This means that anyone who can sign in to your Apple Account, with access to its Keychain, could restore your chats, so protect your Apple Account well. If you do not use iCloud backup and lose your phone, your HopNet identity and history cannot be recovered by anyone, including us.

Permissions the app may ask for

  • Camera: to scan QR codes, take photos and videos to send, and for video calls.
  • Microphone: for voice and video calls and voice notes.
  • Photos: to send the photos and videos you choose.
  • Notifications: to tell you about new messages and incoming calls.
  • Local Network: to connect directly to your contacts' phones on the same Wi-Fi.
  • Face ID: for App Lock, if you turn it on. Face data never leaves Apple's secure system.

Each permission is used only for its stated purpose, on your phone.

Reports, feedback and diagnostics

  • Reporting a contact (chat menu → Report contact) blocks the contact and prepares an email to our abuse team in your own email app. It contains the contact's name as you saved it, their HopNet ID, the date, your reason if you give one, and the recent messages you received from that contact, so we can review them. Nothing goes through our servers, and nothing is sent until you tap send. We use reports only to handle the abuse described in our Terms.
  • Send Feedback (Settings) prepares an email to support@hopnetapp.com in your own email app, with your app and iOS version if you leave that option on. If you choose to attach the diagnostics log, it is encrypted so only HopNet support can read it. We keep feedback emails only as long as needed to answer them and improve the app.
  • Otherwise the diagnostics log stays on your phone.
  • If you turned on Apple's "Share With App Developers", Apple may share crash reports with us under Apple's own policy.

Your rights

Because we hold no account or profile about you, there is normally nothing for us to give you, correct or delete: your data is on your phone, and you control it. If you have emailed us, you can ask us to delete that correspondence at privacy@hopnetapp.com. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

HopNet is intended for people aged 17 and over and is not directed to children.
